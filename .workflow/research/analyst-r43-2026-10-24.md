## 3. Rulings requested in the brief

### (a) ROA 11.00 vs 10.53
Cause is F1: `calculate_roa` rounds the fraction to 2 dp (`precision=2`) before multiplying by 100, so 0.10526 becomes 0.11. Not a bridge bug. The test at `test_r43_computed.py:212-215` documents the defect and pins it. Fix scope (for staff-backend): quantize after x100 (or keep >= 4 dp on the fraction) in `_calculate_growth`, `_calculate_margin`, `_calculate_average_ratio`; replace the hedged assertions with exact values; bumping the affected metrics' `formula_version` is required because the output changes (AGENTS: versioned methodology).

### (c) D-6
- **DPS = abs(dividends_paid) / shares_outstanding: ACCEPT for purification** (the dividend-method needs dividends actually received during the year; cash paid per year-end share is the right quantity), labelled "cash dividends paid per share (derived)", never "declared DPS". Conditions already in R44 hold; add the cap in F4 and the bonus-share flag. The sign is safe (all 628 DB lines negative).
- **DPS as input to `dividend_stability` and `dividend_growing`: REJECT** (F6/F7/F8): timing noise creates false FAILs and gap years are skipped. Keep those two checks NOT_RUN until a declared-DPS source exists (vendor `dps` exists for 122/212, sparse). `dividend_earnings_coverage` (paid DPS / EPS of the same year) may stay but must be labelled "cash paid / EPS" because paid-in-year mostly reflects the prior year's earnings (COMI 23.9 % vs vendor payout 17.9 %). This is a score-check input change; D-7 says no score version bump, so the owner should confirm that treating "paid" as "DPS" does not need `SCORE_METHODOLOGY_VERSION` 1.1.
- **Interest-only purification numerator: ACCEPT only as a partial estimate**, and only after F3 (status `ESTIMATE_PARTIAL`, "at least" wording replaced by "interest and investment income only; 13 checklist lines not covered") and F4 (ratio > 1 -> INSUFFICIENT or capped with reason) are implemented. F5 means the word "at least" must not be used; use "partial".

### (b) ROE/ROA with no prior period: options (user decides; analyst does not pick)

Real-data impact (212 companies): for the latest period, ROE has 120 companies with spot inputs and 120 with average inputs (0 lost); ROA 200 spot vs 199 average (1 lost). Across all periods the average basis loses 124 of 592 ROE periods and 209 of 993 ROA periods, which are almost exactly each company's **oldest** period (history, not the latest score input). The design table (§1.5) said "None allowed -> spot" but the engine rejects None, so design and code disagree today.

| Option | What it does | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Keep INSUFFICIENT_DATA (current) | ROE/ROA only on average capital; oldest period and gap years have no value | One definition for all companies and years; no silent basis mixing; peer percentiles and score thresholds stay comparable; honest | Loses the oldest period of history (affects 5-year checks marginally); 1 company loses its latest ROA |
| B. Fall back to year-end (spot) equity/assets, with a warning | Value shown when the prior period is missing | Full coverage (+1 latest ROA, +about 21 % of historical periods) | Two bases under one metric code; spot is up to 17 % lower for growing firms (COMI ROE spot 26.63 vs average 32.09); mixes into percentiles and thresholds unless the basis is carried everywhere |
| C. Two metrics: `roe`/`roa` strict average, plus `roe_year_end`/`roa_year_end` always computed; score uses average, falls back to year-end only with a stated basis and reduced confidence | Both visible, basis explicit | Coverage plus honesty; UI can show both (D-10 already notes the Ratios tab uses spot) | More rows and UI, a new metric definition and formula version, and a score-input rule to decide |

**Recommendation: A for v1**, because the latest-period coverage loss is about zero (0 of 120 ROE, 1 of 200 ROA) and A is the only option that cannot mix bases. Revisit C if the owner wants history charts. Independently of this choice, F12 (87 companies with no `total_equity` mapping) is the real ROE coverage gap and is much larger (92 of 212 companies lack ROE for the latest period).
